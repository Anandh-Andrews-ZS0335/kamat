A collections manager needs: “Which action has the best chance of improving this member’s outcome, within today’s limits?”

What a prescription considers

The decision combines risk with the conditions around an action

01

Expected loss

Exposure, probability of default and loss given default show what is at stake.

02

Incremental impact

Uplift estimates whether a specific action changes the likelihood of cure compared with no action.

03

Action fit

Recent payment history, delinquency, prior contacts and response patterns help select SMS, call, plan or hardship support.

04

Member context

Consent, contact preference, payment-plan eligibility and vulnerability signals determine what may be appropriate.

The economic decision

Each eligible option gets a value estimate

Action value = uplift × expected loss − action cost

The planner compares actions for each account. It favours the action expected to make the most meaningful difference, rather than simply contacting the highest-risk account first.

Expected loss = 12-month PD × exposure × LGD. Uplift uses observed historical outcomes after 30 days.

Constraints are part of the decision

A useful recommendation must also be permissible and deliverable

✓

Consent and suppression

SMS and calls require the relevant consent. Do-not-contact, deceased, bankruptcy, litigation and fraud-hold records stay out of the queue.

✓

Contact policy

Each member receives at most one contact per day, with a seven-attempt limit in the current policy.

✓

Collector capacity

The optimiser allocates available shift minutes across collectors. It cannot create more calls than the team can handle.

✓

Human approval

A manager can approve, edit or reject every recommendation before any action reaches the bank.
